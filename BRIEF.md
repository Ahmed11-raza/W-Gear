# 05 — TYPOGRAPHY

Typography must feel expensive.

Do not use:

* Arial
* Roboto
* default system stacks
* generic Bootstrap typography
* visually boring typography

Do not automatically use Inter simply because it is convenient.

Preferred display directions:

* Satoshi
* Cabinet Grotesk
* Neue Montreal
* Geist
* refined grotesque
* carefully selected condensed grotesque

You may combine:

### Display

A strong grotesque or slightly condensed grotesque.

Large.

Confident.

Editorial.

### UI

Neutral grotesque.

Highly readable.

Functional.

### Technical

Monospace only where it creates meaning:

* GSM
* measurements
* fabric properties
* garment dimensions
* production specifications
* player numbers
* manufacturing data

Do not sprinkle monospace everywhere just to look "technical."

### Typography principles

Use aggressive scale jumps.

Create visual hierarchy through size rather than excessive decoration.

Hero H1:

Maximum 2–3 lines.

Prefer a powerful short statement.

Never create a 6-line hero headline.

Avoid centered-everything syndrome.

Use wide editorial containers.

Typography itself should become part of the visual identity.

# 07 — HERO: THE SIGNATURE MOMENT

The hero is the most important visual moment on the website.

Do NOT simply place a person on the right and text on the left.

Think like a campaign art director.

Create a cinematic, high-impact composition.

Potential direction:

A full-viewport moving athlete captured during real movement.

Fabric visibly reacts to motion.

Lighting reveals garment construction.

Typography interacts with the photograph rather than sitting inside a standard text box.

Possible headline territory:

> MADE TO MOVE.
> BUILT FOR EVERYDAY.

OR

> MORE THAN SPORTSWEAR.

OR invent something better.

Do not automatically use those lines.

Choose the strongest proposition after considering the entire brand.

### Hero principles

* One dominant visual idea
* One dominant headline
* One primary CTA
* One secondary CTA
* Minimal UI noise
* No badge explosion
* No fake statistics
* No "trusted by 10,000+ athletes"

**Who is it for?**

**Why should I care?**

**Where do I go next?**

# 54 — MARKET ASSUMPTIONS

The supplied research suggests a premium urban Pakistani audience, particularly customers already familiar with international premium sportswear, and identifies mobile-first behavior, COD, WhatsApp, sizing, and payment friction as important design considerations.

Use those findings as **working product hypotheses**, not as public-facing claims.

If a numerical market claim is not independently verified, do not display it on the website.

Do not write:

"82% of our customers..."

unless real data exists.

Do not write:

"WhatsApp increases conversion by 25%"

unless that is verified for this business.

The design should respond to the hypotheses without advertising them as facts.

The brand should visually occupy the space between:

**cheap local sportswear**

Do not communicate this with loud price-comparison messaging.

* construction detail

* digital experience
* restrained branding

The perception should be:

> **export-grade quality without imported-brand pricing.**

# 60 — THE ANTI-TEMPLATE RULE

Every major visual decision must answer:

**Why does this belong to THIS brand?**

If the same design could be used for:

* a coffee shop
* a SaaS startup
* a furniture store
* a skincare brand

then it is probably too generic.

The website must communicate:

**movement + sportswear + modern Pakistan + premium quality + team identity.**

# 61 — THE ANTI-AI RULE

At every stage ask:

> "Would an experienced human art director actually approve this?"

If not:

Change it.

Do not settle for the first plausible layout.

Generate alternatives internally.

Remove unnecessary visual noise.

Prefer one extraordinary visual idea over ten mediocre effects.

Build a sportswear brand that feels like it belongs to the future of Pakistani athletic culture.

Not a local store pretending to be Nike.
